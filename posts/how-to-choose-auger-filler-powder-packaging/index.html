How to Choose an Auger Filler for Powder Packaging

A technical selection guide: how auger dosing works, how to match the screw to your powder and dose, and what to check before you order.

An auger filler is the dosing unit at the heart of most powder packaging lines. A rotating screw — the auger — turns inside a tube above the pack, and each turn moves a fixed volume of powder into it. Get it right and the line fills consistently; get it wrong and the dose drifts, powder bridges, or dust escapes at the filling point. This guide explains how to choose one: how auger dosing works, what your powder says about the screw you need, and what to check before you order.
It is written for plant managers, production managers and packaging engineers comparing machines. The figures come from PACKGENIX powder machines — the PBF-220, PBF-380, PBF-250, PBF-06 and PBF-09. If you are still choosing the machine type, start with our guide to choosing a powder packaging machine.

1. What Auger Filling Is

Auger filling is volumetric dosing: a helical screw turning inside a close-fitting tube. Powder from the hopper falls into the screw’s flights, and as the screw rotates it carries the powder forward and discharges it at the bottom of the tube into the pack below. The dose is set by how far the screw turns: a set number of revolutions delivers a set volume.
Because the dose is a volume, its weight depends on the bulk density of the powder — the weight of a given volume. Two powders filled with the same setting can weigh different amounts, which is why calibration with the actual product matters. Between doses the auger stops while the filled pack is sealed or indexed away.
The same principle serves very different formats: a sachet formed from roll film, a VFFS bag, or a premade pouch held open under the filling head. What changes is the machine around the auger, not the dosing principle.

2. Why Auger Dosing Is Used for Powders

Auger dosing is the standard choice for dry powders because it handles a wide range of powder characters with one mechanism. Fine, free-flowing and in-between powders can all be dosed by the same screw-and-tube arrangement, provided the screw is matched to the powder. The same number of revolutions delivers the same volume, dose after dose.
It also fits the formats powder buyers use: auger fillers sit at the centre of sachet, VFFS and premade pouch lines alike, so the pack format stays your choice. And because the dose is set by revolutions, changing it is a setting change, within the range the auger and machine are built for.

3. Powder Flowability and Bulk Density

Two properties of your powder shape almost every auger decision: flowability and bulk density. Flowability is how freely the powder moves. A free-flowing powder fills the flights the same way every time. A cohesive powder — one whose particles cling together — moves unevenly, can bridge across the hopper, and can leave the flights partly filled, so the dose varies.
Bulk density is the weight of a given volume of powder. A light, fluffy powder needs a larger volume to reach the same weight as a dense one, so the auger must turn further for the same dose. Send your bulk density with your inquiry if you know it; if not, a sample test will establish it. For more on how these properties change filling, see our guide to how powder flowability affects filling and packaging.

4. Screw / Auger Size

The auger is sized to the dose and the powder. What matters is screw diameter and pitch — the distance between flights. A larger diameter and coarser pitch move more powder per revolution, suiting large doses; a smaller diameter and finer pitch move less, giving finer control over small doses. A screw sized for 350 g will not dose 5 g accurately, and one sized for 5 g takes too many revolutions to fill 350 g.
The powder matters as much as the dose. A screw that doses a free-flowing powder cleanly may not suit a fine, cohesive powder that packs into the flights. Confirm which screw sizes are available for your powder and dose range, and expect a change of powder to mean a review of the screw.

Check the filling range first. The machine must cover your target dose with room on either side — your smallest and largest dose, not only your most common one. The PACKGENIX powder machines cover:

  • PBF-380 — Automatic Auger Powder Sachet Packing Machine — filling 1–350 g.
  • PBF-250 — Automatic Powder VFFS Packing Machine — filling 1–100 g.
  • PBF-06 — Automatic Auger Powder VFFS Packing Machine with Dust Collection — filling 150–2000 ml.

Note that the PBF-06 is rated in millilitres, because its larger doses are quoted by volume, while the sachet and small-bag machines are rated in grams. A machine rated far above your dose will struggle with small amounts, and one rated below it cannot reach your target at all.

6. Hopper Design

The hopper holds the powder above the auger and feeds it by gravity into the screw. If the feed is uneven, the dose is uneven, so the hopper is part of the dosing system, not just a container.
Two common feeding problems are bridging and rat-holing. Bridging is when powder forms an arch across the hopper and stops flowing. Rat-holing is when powder flows down a narrow central channel while powder at the sides stays put. Both starve the auger, so discuss them with the supplier for your powder.
Hopper capacity sets how often the line needs refilling. The PBF-220 carries a hopper of around 10 L. Hopper size is a machine-by-machine figure, so confirm it for any machine you compare.

7. Feeding Consistency and Dosing Repeatability

Repeatability — how closely each dose matches the last — depends on the flowability of the powder, uniform feeding from the hopper, and calibration with the actual product. A free-flowing powder fed evenly doses more repeatably than a cohesive powder fed unevenly, on the same machine. A sample test with your powder tells you more than a specification sheet alone.
Compare machines only on published accuracy figures, and treat the absence of a figure as a gap, not as a promise. Among the PACKGENIX powder machines, the only verified filling-accuracy figure is for the PBF-09, rated at ±0.3–1.5%. No accuracy figure is verified for the PBF-06, PBF-220, PBF-250 or PBF-380, so do not compare those machines on accuracy claims — ask for a sample test with your powder instead.

8. Dust

Auger filling of fine powders creates dust where powder drops from the tube into the pack. Dust settles on sealing surfaces and can weaken the seal, it coats the machine and the working area, and product is lost to the air instead of going into the pack. Fine, light powders dust far more than coarse, dense ones.
Where dust control is fitted, it extracts dust at the filling point. The PBF-06 is fitted with dust collection and the PBF-09 with dust extraction. Dust control is not fitted on every powder machine, so if your powder is dusty, confirm it is fitted on the machine you choose. For the full picture, see our guide to reducing dust in powder packaging.

9. Calibration

Calibration turns a volumetric dose into a correct weight. Because the auger delivers a volume, the same setting gives a different weight for every powder, and even for different batches of the same powder if the bulk density shifts. Calibration means running your actual product through the machine, weighing the doses, and adjusting the revolutions until the weight is right.
Do this before you commit to a machine, not after it arrives. A sample test establishes the auger size, the dose weight and the repeatability you can expect, and it surfaces problems — bridging, dust, poor sealing — while there is still time to change the configuration.

10. Why One Auger Is Not Suitable for Every Powder

An auger is not a universal part. Dose size, powder character and pack format all change the screw that suits the job: a small dose needs a small screw and a large dose a large one.
Treat the auger as part of the quotation, not as an accessory. Tell the supplier your powder type, dose range and pack format, ask which screw sizes are offered, and confirm what happens if you add a second powder later.

12. Conclusion

Choosing an auger filler starts with the powder, not the machine. Establish the dose range, the flowability and bulk density of your powder, and the pack format, then match the auger, hopper and dust control to those facts — and confirm the result with a sample test before you order.
