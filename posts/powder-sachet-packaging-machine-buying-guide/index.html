Powder Sachet Packaging Machine Buying Guide

How to compare powder sachet machines by filling weight, sachet size, seal format, film and speed — and what to send for a quotation.

A powder sachet packaging machine forms small single-serve sachets from roll film, fills each one with a measured dose of powder, and seals it — all in one automatic cycle. It is the machine behind single-serve coffee, seasoning sachets, detergent samples, pharmaceutical doses and many other small powder packs. This buying guide is for plant managers, production managers and procurement teams comparing sachet machines. It covers filling weight, sachet size, seal formats, film, speed and the information a supplier needs before quoting.
If you have not yet settled on sachets over other powder formats, start with our guide to choosing a powder packaging machine, which compares sachet, VFFS and premade pouch lines. This guide assumes the decision is made: you want sachets, and you want to buy the right machine for them.

1. When Sachet Packaging Is Suitable

Sachets suit small, single-serve doses. A sachet gives one portion: a single cup of coffee, one wash of detergent, one dose of a pharmaceutical powder, one sample of a new product. The buyer gets portion control and hygiene, and the producer gets a pack that is cheap to make, easy to distribute and simple to open.
Sachets also suit sampling and trial sizes. A small sachet is a low-cost way to put a product in a customer’s hand, and a practical format for products sold by the portion rather than by the kilo. If your dose is small, your product is sold by the serving, or you need a sample format, a sachet line is worth comparing against larger bags and pouches. If your dose is large or your product is sold in bulk, a VFFS bag or a premade pouch will usually fit better.

2. Filling Weight

The first number to fix is the dose. The PBF-220 fills 1–100 g per sachet, and the PBF-380 fills 1–350 g per bag. Check your smallest and largest dose against the machine’s range, not only your most common dose: a machine that covers your average but not your extremes will force a compromise on one of your products.
Both machines dose by auger, which suits powder sachets because it delivers a consistent volumetric dose at sachet speeds. For how the screw, the powder and calibration affect the dose, see our guide to choosing an auger filler for powder packaging.

3. Bag Width and Length

Sachet size is the second number to fix. The PBF-220 forms sachets of 20–145 x 20–110 mm, and the PBF-380 forms bags of 14–150 x 50–200 mm. Width is set by the film and the forming tube; length is set by the sealing jaws and the feed. Confirm that your sachet dimensions fall inside the machine’s range, including any format change you plan later — a machine bought for one size that cannot grow to the next is a short-lived purchase.
Send the supplier your sachet dimensions with your inquiry, not just the filling weight. The two numbers together decide whether the machine fits your product, and they are the first thing a supplier checks.

4. Seal Formats: Back Seal, 3-Side Seal, 4-Side Seal

A sachet can be sealed in three formats. A back seal — also called a pillow or fin seal — joins the film along the back of the sachet and seals the top and bottom, giving a familiar pillow-shaped pack. A 3-side seal closes three edges of a flat sachet, which is sealed across the top after filling. A 4-side seal closes all four edges, giving a flat sachet with neat sealed edges on every side.
The PBF-220 supports back seal, 3-side seal and 4-side seal formats, so the format is a choice rather than a limitation. Which format suits your product depends on appearance, how the sachet is opened, and how it is packed for distribution. For a detailed comparison of the three formats — appearance, seal strength, film use and which products suit each — see our comparison of back seal, 3-side seal and 4-side seal sachets.

5. Film Selection

The film decides whether the seal holds. Film type and thickness affect how the film seals, how strong the seal is, and how well the sachet protects the powder. A film that seals well on one machine may need different sealing settings on another, and a film chosen for appearance alone may not seal reliably at production speed.
Confirm with the supplier which films the machine is built for, and test your actual film on the machine before you order. Send a roll of your film with your product sample if you can: the sealing test is part of the sample test, and it is the only way to be sure the film, the machine and the powder work together.

6. Auger Dosing

Powder sachets are dosed by auger. A rotating screw delivers a set volume of powder into each sachet, which gives a consistent dose at the speeds a sachet line runs, and handles the fine powders that sachets usually carry. The dose is set by how far the screw turns, so changing the dose is a setting change within the range the auger is built for.
The auger has to match the powder and the dose: screw size, hopper feeding and calibration all affect whether the dose weight holds across a run. For the full selection guide, see our guide to choosing an auger filler for powder packaging.

7. Speed

The PBF-220 runs at around 40–100 sachets per minute, and the PBF-380 at around 20–60 bags per minute. Speed depends on the dose, the powder and the sachet size: a large dose or a difficult powder runs slower than a small, free-flowing one, so treat the top of the range as a best case rather than a promise.
Quote your target output in sachets per minute and per shift, and confirm it against your largest dose, not your smallest. A machine that meets your output at 5 g may not meet it at 100 g, and the gap only shows up when the line is running your real product.

8. Date Coding

A date coder prints the production date, batch code or expiry date on each sachet as it is formed. On the PBF-220, a date coder is available as an option — it is not standard equipment, so say in your inquiry whether you need it. If your market or your customers require a date or a batch code on every sachet, confirm the coder is included in the quotation rather than assuming it.
Check too where the code prints on the sachet and whether it stays readable on your film. A code that smudges or lands on a seal is no use, and that is another detail the sample test can settle.

9. Automation Options

Before you compare options, settle what happens around the machine. How does powder reach the hopper — by hand, or from upstream equipment? How are finished sachets collected — into a bin, onto a conveyor, or into a counting and packing station? And does the sachet machine need to connect to equipment before or after it on the line?
Put these answers in your inquiry. A supplier can only quote the right configuration if it knows what the machine connects to, and an option that looks useful on a list may not be the one your line actually needs. Ask what is included as standard and what is optional, and get the answer in the quotation.

10. PBF-220 vs PBF-380

Both machines are automatic auger sachet machines from PACKGENIX, and the choice between them comes down to dose and speed. The differences, on verified figures, are:
  • PBF-220 — High-Speed Automatic Powder Sachet Packing Machine — around 40–100 sachets per minute; filling 1–100 g; sachets 20–145 x 20–110 mm; back seal, 3-side seal and 4-side seal; around 2 kW; hopper around 10 L.
  • PBF-380 — Automatic Auger Powder Sachet Packing Machine — around 20–60 bags per minute; filling 1–350 g; bags 14–150 x 50–200 mm.
In short: the PBF-220 is the higher-speed sachet machine for doses up to 100 g, while the PBF-380 covers larger doses up to 350 g at a lower speed. Choose by dose first, then by speed — and if your dose range spans both machines, say so in your inquiry.
Both machines are listed in the sachet packaging machines category, and the powder packaging solutions application page shows how sachet lines fit the wider powder range.

11. Information Needed Before Quotation

A supplier cannot quote a sachet machine from a product name alone. Send these five details with your inquiry:
  • Powder type — what the powder is, and how it flows — free-flowing or cohesive, fine or coarse.
  • Filling weight — the dose per sachet, smallest and largest.
  • Sachet dimensions — width and length of the sachet you want.
  • Seal format — back seal, 3-side seal or 4-side seal.
  • Target speed — sachets per minute, and per shift if you know it.
Add a product sample and a roll of your film if you can. With those, the supplier can run a sample test and confirm the auger, the seal and the speed on your product rather than on a specification sheet.

12. Conclusion

A good sachet machine purchase is decided before the quotation: dose, sachet size, seal format, film and target speed, confirmed against the machine’s verified range and tested with your powder and film. The PBF-220 suits high-speed sachets up to 100 g; the PBF-380 suits larger doses up to 350 g. Send the five details above, and the quotation you get back will be for a machine that fits your product — not a machine that nearly does.
