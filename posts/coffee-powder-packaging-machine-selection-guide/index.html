Coffee Powder Packaging Machine Selection Guide

Coffee powder is one of the most common powders packed on sachet, VFFS and premade pouch lines. This guide explains how to select a coffee powder packaging machine by filling weight, pack format, dosing, sealing, dust and speed.

Coffee powder is one of the most common powders packed on sachet, vertical form-fill-seal and premade pouch lines. It is sold as single-serve sachets, retail bags and larger pouches, and each of those packs is made on a different type of machine. Choosing a coffee powder packaging machine therefore means choosing a pack format first, then a dosing system, a sealing configuration and a speed that match your product and your output. This guide walks through those decisions using the verified specifications of the PACKGENIX powder machines.

1. Introduction

Coffee — instant, ground or blended — has similar packaging requirements across its forms: a controlled filling weight, a clean seal, and a pack that protects the powder until it is opened. The machines in this guide are auger filling machines, which suit fine powders, and they cover the three main routes for coffee powder: sachets, vertical form-fill-seal bags and premade pouches.
For the general selection factors, see our guide to choosing a powder packaging machine, which covers the general selection factors. This guide applies those factors specifically to coffee powder.

2. Coffee Powder Characteristics

Coffee powder is fine and dusty, and depending on the grind it ranges from free-flowing to semi-free-flowing. That matters because the way a powder flows decides how it can be dosed: a free-flowing powder feeds evenly through an auger, while a powder that flows poorly needs more care in the hopper and the dosing system. For a fuller explanation of flow behaviour, see how powder flowability affects filling and packaging.
Coffee powder is also aroma-sensitive, so the film or pouch material and the quality of the seal are part of the machine decision. Coffee powder is dusty too: fine particles escape during filling and can settle on sealing surfaces, which is why dust handling is covered separately later in this guide.

3. Filling Weight

Filling weight is the first specification to fix, because it decides which machines can do the job. The verified filling ranges of the PACKGENIX powder machines are: the PBF-220 covers 1–100 g, the PBF-380 covers 1–350 g and the PBF-250 covers 1–100 g. Choose a machine whose verified range covers your target weight with room on both sides — a machine running at the very edge of its range is harder to keep accurate and stable.
Single-serve portions sit at the small end of these ranges, while retail bags and larger packs use the upper end. Confirm your own target weight, in grams, against the verified range of the machine you are considering. If your range spans small single serves to large retail packs, you may need more than one machine, or a machine with a wider range such as the PBF-380.

4. Sachet vs VFFS vs Premade Pouch

Coffee powder is packed on three routes, and each one suits a different product and market. A sachet line makes small, flat or pillow-style single-serve packs. A vertical form-fill-seal (VFFS) line makes retail bags from a roll of film. A premade pouch line fills pouches that are already formed, which suits stand-up retail pouches with a finished look.
  • Sachet — PBF-220 and PBF-380 — single-serve coffee portions; PBF-220 fills 1–100 g at approximately 40–100 sachets per minute, PBF-380 fills 1–350 g at approximately 20–60 bags per minute.
  • VFFS — PBF-250 — retail coffee bags; fills 1–100 g at 10–50 bags per minute, with a bag size of 30–150 × 30–110 mm.
  • Premade pouch — PBF-09 — stand-up coffee pouches; approximately 20–30 pcs per minute, pouch size 60–220 × 80–310 mm, with a filling accuracy of ±0.3–1.5%.
For a detailed comparison of the VFFS and premade pouch routes, see VFFS vs premade pouch powder packaging. For the sachet route specifically, see the powder sachet packaging machine buying guide.

5. Auger Dosing

Auger dosing suits coffee powder because it meters a fine powder by volume. A screw — the auger — rotates inside a tube, and each rotation delivers a controlled volume of powder into the pack. Changing the auger size or the number of rotations changes the dose, which is how the same machine covers a filling range such as 1–100 g. The dose is set on the machine controls, so changeover between filling weights is a settings change plus an auger change where needed.
Auger dosing works best with powders that flow evenly, so check the flowability of your coffee powder before the machine is selected. For more on auger fillers, see how to choose an auger filler for powder packaging.

6. Film and Pouch Format

Coffee powder is packed in heat-sealable laminated film on sachet and VFFS lines, and in premade pouches on pouch lines. The film or pouch protects the powder from air and moisture and carries the branding, so the material should be chosen together with the machine, not after it. On the PBF-220, the film structures used include heat-sealable laminated films, and the final film specification should be confirmed before the machine is produced.

7. Sealing

Coffee sachets are sealed in one of three formats: back seal, 3-side seal or 4-side seal. A back seal sachet has a longitudinal seam down the back and a clean front face. A 3-side seal sachet is folded on one edge and sealed on the other three. A 4-side seal sachet is sealed on all four edges. The format changes the appearance of the pack, the printing area and the amount of film used for seals, and it is selected as a machine configuration.
Both the PBF-220 and the PBF-380 can be configured for back, three-side and four-side sealing. For a full comparison of the three formats, see back seal vs 3-side seal vs 4-side seal sachets.

8. Dust

Coffee powder is dusty, and dust is a practical problem on a packing line, not only a housekeeping one. Fine powder escapes during filling, settles on sealing jaws and can weaken seals or mark the outside of the pack. Dust in the sealing area is a common cause of leaking sachets, so check how a machine handles dust before you select it.
Dust extraction is fitted on some machines and not on others. The PBF-09 premade pouch machine has dust extraction. Do not assume that every powder machine has it: the PBF-220, PBF-380 and PBF-250 are not specified with dust collection, so if dust control is important for your coffee line, raise it with your supplier and confirm what is fitted before ordering. For practical steps, see how to reduce dust in powder packaging.

9. Speed

Speed should be compared on verified figures, for the same pack size and filling weight. The verified speeds of the machines in this guide are: PBF-220 approximately 40–100 sachets per minute, PBF-380 approximately 20–60 bags per minute, PBF-250 10–50 bags per minute, and PBF-09 approximately 20–30 pcs per minute. The fastest machine is not automatically the right one — a sachet line and a premade pouch line run at different speeds because they make different packs.

10. Date Coding

Date coding prints the production date, and where required the batch number or shelf life, on the pack during the packaging process. On the PBF-220, date coding is available as an option for production date printing on sachets. If your market requires date or batch marking on every pack, specify the coder as part of the machine order, so it is fitted and tested with the sealing and cutting stations.

All four machines are listed under the powder packaging solutions application page, in the sachet packaging machines, VFFS packaging machines and premade pouch packaging machines categories.

12. Information Required Before Quotation

Prepare the following before you ask for a quote, and the recommendation will be faster and more accurate.
  • Coffee type — instant, ground or blended coffee powder, and the grind size if known.
  • Filling weight — target weight in grams, and the range if more than one pack size is planned.
  • Package dimensions — sachet, bag or pouch size, in millimetres.
  • Seal format — back seal, 3-side seal or 4-side seal for sachets; pouch format for premade pouches.
  • Target speed — packs per minute required, for the pack size and filling weight above.
  • Film or pouch — film structure or pouch specification if already chosen, or ask for a recommendation.
  • Date coding — whether production date, batch or shelf-life printing is required on the pack.

13. Conclusion

Selecting a coffee powder packaging machine comes down to five decisions: the pack format, the filling weight, the dosing system, the seal format and the speed. Sachets suit single serves, VFFS suits retail bags and premade pouches suit stand-up retail packs. Auger dosing suits fine, free-flowing coffee powder. The seal format is chosen for appearance and branding, dust handling is confirmed per machine, and speed is compared on verified figures for your own pack.
With those five decisions made, the choice narrows quickly: the PBF-220 and PBF-380 for sachets, the PBF-250 for VFFS bags and the PBF-09 for premade pouches.
