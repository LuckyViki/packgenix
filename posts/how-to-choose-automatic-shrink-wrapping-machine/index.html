How to Choose an Automatic Shrink Wrapping Machine

A practical buyer’s guide for selecting automatic shrink wrapping machines by product size, film type, line speed, sealing style, and factory integration needs.

An automatic shrink wrapping machine should be selected from the product, not from the catalog. The right machine depends on what you pack, how you pack it, what film you use and how fast your line runs. This guide walks through the ten decisions that determine whether a shrink wrapping line runs smoothly or becomes a bottleneck.

1. Confirm Product Size and Shape

Start with the largest single product and the largest bundle you plan to wrap. Record the length, width and height of each product, and note whether it is a bottle, can, carton, jar or an irregular shape. Round or slippery containers need stable conveying and even film tension, while tall or top-heavy products need a tunnel and conveyor that keep the pack upright through the shrink process.
The sealing area and the tunnel opening must fit the largest pack with clearance on all sides. A machine sized only for your smallest product will force you to rework the line when a larger format is added.

2. Confirm Pack Combination

Decide the bundle before you decide the machine. Common combinations include 2 x 3, 3 x 4, 3 x 5 and 4 x 6, but the right combination depends on your container size, carton size and pallet pattern. The bundle must be stable enough to survive transport without shifting, and neat enough for the next step, whether that is cartoning, palletizing or direct dispatch.
Confirm the combination with your customer or distributor when the pack is sold as a retail multipack. A bundle that looks right on the line but fails on a retail shelf is an expensive mistake to correct.

3. Choose Film Type

PE film is the usual choice for heavier transport bundles such as paint cans, aerosol cans, bottled drinks and cartons, because it holds the bundle together under load. POF film is the usual choice for lighter retail packs where a clear, tight finish matters more than holding force.
Film choice changes the machine setup, including sealing temperature, tunnel temperature and conveyor speed, so confirm the film before the machine is configured. For a detailed comparison, see PE vs POF Shrink Film: How to Match Film with Packaging Machines.

4. Define Required Line Speed

State the required output in packs per minute, not in vague terms. Count the packs your filling or cartoning line actually produces, and allow for changeovers and short stops. A wrapping machine that cannot keep up becomes the bottleneck for the whole line, while a machine bought far above the real output wastes floor space, power and budget.
Ask the supplier to confirm the rated speed for your exact product and pack combination, not for a smaller or lighter test pack. Speed should be quoted for your product, your film and your bundle.

5. Select Sealing Method

Most automatic lines use an L-type sealer, which seals two sides of the film around the pack before it enters the heat tunnel. Check that the sealing blade length covers your largest pack, and that the seal is strong enough for the pack weight. Weak seals split in transport, especially on heavy or sharp-edged products.
Confirm whether the machine uses a single sealing station or a side sealer, and whether the sealing temperature can be adjusted for different film types and thicknesses. A machine that only suits one film limits your future options.

6. Check Shrink Tunnel Size and Heating Capacity

The tunnel must be long and wide enough for the largest pack, and its heating capacity must match the film and the line speed. If the tunnel is too short or too cool, the film stays loose and wrinkled. If it is too hot, the film can burn, split or damage heat-sensitive products.
Look for even airflow and stable temperature control across the tunnel. Ask how the temperature is set and monitored, and whether the tunnel can hold a stable temperature during a full production shift.

7. Consider Upstream and Downstream Integration

A shrink wrapping machine rarely works alone. It usually sits between filling, capping or labeling upstream and cartoning, palletizing or dispatch downstream. Confirm the conveyor height, conveyor direction and pack spacing at both ends, so the machine fits the line without extra transfer equipment.
Check the control interface as well. The wrapping machine should start, stop and signal faults in step with the rest of the line, rather than running as an isolated unit that needs a dedicated operator watching it.

8. Confirm Voltage and Factory Conditions

Confirm the power supply, compressed air supply and floor space before the machine is ordered. Note the voltage and frequency at your factory, the available compressed air pressure and flow, and the space needed for film loading, maintenance access and pack infeed and outfeed.
As an example, the Packgenix PMB-08 runs on 380 V, 50 Hz, 23 kW, needs compressed air at 0.6–0.8 MPa with a flow of 0.3 m³/min, and weighs 1000 kg. Check the requirements of any machine against your factory supply before you commit, not after delivery.

9. Prepare Information for Quotation

A supplier can only recommend the right machine when the product details are clear. Prepare the product dimensions and weight, the pack combination, the film type and thickness, the target output per minute, the power supply and the available floor space. Product samples or photos help the supplier check details that a data sheet cannot show.
Also state your target delivery time and the level of installation and training support you need. A quotation based on complete information is far more accurate than one based on a product name alone.
