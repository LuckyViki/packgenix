Back Seal vs 3-Side Seal vs 4-Side Seal Sachets

One sachet machine can produce three different sachet formats. This guide compares back seal, 3-side seal and 4-side seal sachets so you can choose the format that fits your powder, your branding and your line.

A sachet is defined as much by its seals as by the powder inside it. On a sachet packing line, the same powder, the same filling weight and the same film can come out as three different packs — a back seal sachet, a 3-side seal sachet or a 4-side seal sachet — depending on how the film is folded and sealed. The format changes how the sachet looks on the shelf, how much film it uses, where the branding sits and how it is handled after packing. This guide compares the three sachet seal types side by side, using the formats produced by the PACKGENIX sachet machines, so you can match the format to your product instead of leaving the decision to chance.

1. Introduction

Choosing a seal format is a packaging decision, not a machine decision. A single sachet machine can be configured for more than one format, which means the format should be selected for the product, the brand and the way the sachet will be stored, shipped and opened — not simply because it is the default setting on the machine. The sections below explain how each format is formed and where the practical differences lie.
The comparison covers appearance, sealing area, film use, printing area and pack geometry, followed by typical powder applications and the machine configurations that produce each format. For the wider question of how to choose a powder packaging machine, see our powder packaging machine selection guide.

2. What Is a Back Seal Sachet

A back seal sachet, also called a centre seal or fin seal sachet, is formed from a flat film web that is folded into a tube. The two long edges of the film are brought together at the back of the tube and sealed into a single longitudinal seam — the fin, or back seal — that runs the full length of the sachet. The top and bottom of the tube are then sealed and cut, closing the pack.
Because the join sits at the back, the front face of the sachet is one continuous, uninterrupted panel of film. The sachet has a slightly rounded, tube-like body rather than a fully flat profile. They are produced on vertical form-fill-seal sachet lines from a single roll of film.

3. What Is a 3-Side Seal Sachet

A 3-side seal sachet starts with a film web folded in half, so one long edge of the finished sachet is the fold itself. The other three edges — top, bottom and one side — are heat sealed. The result is a flat, rectangular sachet made from two layers of film, with a sealed border on three sides and a clean folded edge on the fourth.
The flat profile is the defining feature of this format. A 3-side seal sachet lies flat, stacks neatly and takes up little space in a carton. It suits single-serve portions of coffee, milk powder, seasoning and beverage powder.

4. What Is a 4-Side Seal Sachet

A 4-side seal sachet is sealed on all four edges. The film is formed so that the top, bottom, left and right edges are all heat sealed, leaving the printed or plain panel framed by a sealed border on every side. There is no folded edge and no back fin — the seal runs around the full perimeter of the sachet.
This format gives the most enclosed, portion-style appearance of the three. The four sealed edges create a neat, symmetrical rectangle that reads as a single portion, which is why 4-side seal sachets are widely used for single-serve samples, sachets packed inside a larger carton, and products where a tidy, fully sealed edge is part of the presentation.

5. Appearance

On the shelf, the three formats look clearly different. A back seal sachet has a rounded body and a clean front face, with the seam hidden at the back. A 3-side seal sachet is flat and rectangular, with sealed edges visible on three sides and a smooth fold on the fourth. A 4-side seal sachet is flat and fully framed, with a sealed border running around all four sides.

6. Sealing Area

The sealing area is the part of the film that is used to close the pack rather than to hold the product or carry the print. The three formats differ in how much of the film is committed to seals. A back seal sachet uses one longitudinal back seam plus the top and bottom seals. A 3-side seal sachet uses three edge seals. A 4-side seal sachet uses four edge seals — the largest sealed perimeter of the three formats.

7. Film Use

Film consumption differs between the formats, but only in relative terms. For a sachet of the same finished size, a 4-side seal format generally uses the most film, because all four edges carry a seal allowance. A 3-side seal format uses less, since the folded edge replaces one seal. A back seal format uses the film efficiently for the volume it holds, because the full width of the web forms the tube and only one longitudinal seam is needed.
These are general relationships, not fixed figures: exact film use depends on sachet dimensions, seal widths, film thickness and machine settings. If film cost matters for your project, compare the formats using your own sachet size and seal widths.

8. Printing Area

Printing area is where the three formats differ most visibly. The back seal sachet offers the largest single uninterrupted print surface: the whole front face is available for branding, product information and instructions, while the back carries the fin seal. The 3-side seal sachet prints across both faces, with the design kept clear of the three sealed edges. The 4-side seal sachet prints inside a frame, because the usable area is the centre panel between the four sealed borders.

9. Pack Geometry

Pack geometry decides how the sachet behaves after it leaves the machine. A back seal sachet is slightly rounded and can look fuller for its fill weight, which suits retail display. A 3-side or 4-side seal sachet is flat and of even thickness, which suits stacking, cartoning and packing many sachets into a display box or an outer carton.

10. Typical Powder Applications

All three formats are used for single-serve and small-dose powders. Coffee and beverage powders, milk powder portions, seasoning, nutrition and protein powder samples and single-serve detergent or cleaning powder portions are all commonly packed as sachets, and the format is usually chosen for the portion size, the branding and the way the sachet is sold.
  • Coffee and beverage powder — single-serve portions are commonly packed as flat 3-side or 4-side seal sachets, or as back seal sachets where a larger front panel is wanted.
  • Milk powder and nutrition powder — single portions and samples suit flat sachets that stack and carton easily.
  • Seasoning and spice powder — small-dose sachets in any of the three formats, chosen mainly for portion size and shelf presentation.
  • Single-serve portions generally — flat formats suit sachets packed in multiples inside a carton; back seal suits sachets sold individually on display.

12. Which Format Should You Choose

There is no format that is universally better than the other two. The right choice comes from matching the format to your product, your branding and your handling needs. Use the questions below to narrow it down.
  • Portion and sachet size — flat 3-side and 4-side seal formats suit small single portions; back seal suits sachets where a fuller, rounded pack is wanted.
  • Branding and labelling — back seal gives the largest uninterrupted front panel; 4-side seal gives a framed centre panel for a simple design.
  • Handling and packing — flat formats stack and carton evenly; back seal sachets need more care in cartoning.
  • Film use — 4-side seal generally uses the most film for the same sachet size; confirm the figures for your own dimensions rather than relying on a general rule.
  • Machine — check that your machine is configured for the format you choose — the PBF-220 and PBF-380 both support all three seal styles.

13. Conclusion

Back seal, 3-side seal and 4-side seal sachets differ in how the film is folded and sealed, and those differences show up in appearance, sealing area, film use, printing area and pack geometry. The back seal format gives a rounded pack with a clean front face. The 3-side seal format gives a flat pack with one folded edge. The 4-side seal format gives a flat, fully framed portion pack. None of the three is the best choice for every product — the right format is the one that fits your powder, your portion, your branding and your line.
Once the format is decided, confirm the sachet size, filling weight and target output with your machine supplier. The PBF-220 and PBF-380 both produce all three formats, so the decision is about your product — not about finding a different machine.
